## Context & Intro

Atlas was a prototype, not a shipped product. It is a clear case for scalable UX in high-stakes workflows: contacts, inventory, memo creation, and payoff tracking in one modular system.

### Past Iterations

Several approaches were already in use. Legacy spreadsheets and fragmented tools each held one part of the trade — a jewel list, a singles sheet, a certificate profile, a transaction record, and a ledger — without carrying contacts, inventory, memo, and payoff as one system.

### User Personas

Three users. Three definitions of “good software.”

Through 25+ embedded interviews and direct operational observation in Belgium, we mapped behavior across the trading lifecycle. These personas were not a decorative exercise. Their bottlenecks became the design requirements.

The same database had to serve three profiles, each with a different fluency, working speed, and mental model.

### Alexandra, 70

Senior trader

Relies on established mental models and double-checks physical records against the screen.

High validation, guided workflows, clear error guards, and familiar patterns.

Defensive input blocks and confirmation step-gates.

### Nuno, 32

New trader / accountant

Runs high-volume daily operations and reaches for the keyboard before the mouse.

Speed, batch execution, and frictionless movement of data.

A universal intent-to-action bar and keyboard-driven entry.

### Alex, 38

Gemologist and CEO

Reads inventory trends, manages high-value deals, and cross-checks lab certificates.

Control, deep search, and a choice of how much detail is on screen.

An advanced query builder, custom columns, and drawers that stay out of the way.

### Benchmark case: Diamtrade

Diamtrade was the trading desk already in use: one desktop for the stock list, the dashboard, and data entry.

### Legacy Industry Benchmarks

- High visual clutter and interface density
- Verbose, rigid multi-step data entry
- Steep learning curves with no interaction hierarchy
- High risk of costly operational errors during fast deals

### The Atlas System Vision

- Single database supporting both guided and power-user workflows
- Non-blocking drawers and contextual action bars
- Constrained data language blocks to eliminate entry errors
- Clean visual hierarchy designed for long-session readability

### The design tension

How do you build one database that gives power users speed without letting a less familiar trader write a catastrophic error?

That meant leaving static views behind and designing for predictability. Clear onboarding, explicit feedback for every action, and the same interaction patterns in every module — so a trader learns Atlas once and moves at their own speed.

## Vheny Diamonds: Branding

Visual language, type, and brand frame for a company-wide rebrand — one recognizable system as the business scaled.

### Role

Founding designer — brand identity

### Client

Vheny Diamonds

### Focus

Visual language, type, and brand frame

### The Problem

Brand, product, and web had to move as a single system. Separate refreshes split the purchase story.

### The Solution

One recognizable system, so identity, interface patterns, and the site tell the same story.

### Key Impact

Shared foundations shortened cycles across marketing and product.

Visual language, type, and brand frame for the company-wide rebrand — one recognizable system as Vheny Diamonds scaled. The surfaces were identity, editorial, environment, the marketing site, and a brand film.
